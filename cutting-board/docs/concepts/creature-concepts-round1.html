Folk of the Tallow Fair

Fourteen humanoid creatures for the valley at night: fair folk, villagers, things in the woods and the bandit camp. They span funny to weird and friendly to evil, built like handmade puppets out of wax, cloth, wood and tin. Odd, sometimes grotesque, never gory. Every portrait is drawn in code at 48×64 in the game palette. Click any creature for its sheet.

1 · Mood map

Each creature sits on two axes: funny ↔ weird (do you laugh or tilt your head?) and friendly ↔ evil (do they help you or rob you?). Evil stays on the cheeky side. Nothing in the bottom half should scare you, but you shouldn't trust it either.

4 · Shared design rules

The things that make fourteen odd creatures read as one world, and keep them on the charming side of strange.

One big change

Every creature is the villager with one part swapped: the head (candle, bell, moon, tin can), the legs (stilts, stump, peg), or the back (shell, sack, junk pile). One big change reads in a second through fog and dither. Extra detail turns to mud.

Heads are faction keys

Masks are how people recognise each other in the dark, so a head shows which side someone is on. Villagers wear pale wax and cream. Fair folk wear painted and striped masks. Bandits wear dark wood with antlers or beaks. Woods folk are made of natural materials: bark, moss, shell, moonlight.

Handmade materials

The Mad God lesson is that everything looks made by hand: lumpy wax, patched cloth, carved wood, dented tin, moss. All matte and seamed, with no gloss and no flesh. Texture comes from two-tone dither, not fine detail, so it survives 640×360.

Bring your own light

The only lights are lantern posts, so most creatures carry a light (candle head, shell lantern, firefly jar, stolen lanterns). That makes them landmarks. Light colour signals intent: warm amber means trade or help, flame-orange eye slits mean bandit, cool moon-yellow means strange.

Not too creepy

Every face has a readable, human expression: a smile, a frown, a blush, boredom. No teeth, wounds, eyes in wrong places or twitchy jump-scare motion. Evil reads as greedy, pompous or sneaky. Villains flee, sulk and fall over. When a mask pops off on death, the face under it is a plain button-eyed cloth face.

Odd proportions, honest scale

Stop-motion puppets have heads too big, legs too short and hands too heavy. Push proportions per creature (Mother Hob is round, Signor Stilts is too tall, the Magpie Kin are child-sized), but keep feet on the ground and eye-lines near the villager's so camera and collision still work.
