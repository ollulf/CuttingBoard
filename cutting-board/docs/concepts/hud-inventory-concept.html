CUTTINGBOARD // UI CONCEPT // TALLOW FAIR

HUD & Inventory

A concept for the player's vitals, the in-game HUD and the inventory screen, laid out after Oblivion and the DayZ mod for Arma 2, drawn in the game's own Tallow Fair palette and Cubix fonts. Both mocks below run on a 640×360 canvas, the game's retro render size, over real frames from the current build. Everything is interactive.

The first twelve are the Tallow Fair palette from psx_palette.gdshader. The rest are colours the UI scenes already use. The one new colour is blood, a darker shade of the inventory's existing "can't drop here" red, because the night palette has no red at all.

1 · The HUD

The HUD keeps the screen clear and stays in the corners. Vitals sit bottom-left like Oblivion's bars. The compass and the hotbar share the bottom centre. DayZ-style status icons line the right edge and stay quiet until something goes wrong. Move the mouse over the frame to turn the compass.

2 · The Inventory

The screen follows the DayZ gear screen, with what is near you on the left and what you carry on the right, wrapped around an Oblivion/Diablo character in the middle. The grids are the game's real ones, a 6×5 chest and the player's 6×8 pack, and the items keep their real grid_size footprints.

Try it: hover an item for its tooltip · drag between the chest, the ground, your pack, the equipment slots and the hands · right-click (or press R) to turn an item, even mid-drag · drop on a hotbar square to link it · drag an item out of the window to drop it on the ground.

Rules the mock follows

One pixel grid. All HUD geometry sits on the 640×360 render grid, and Cubix is set at 8px, where one font pixel equals one screen pixel. Titles use Cubix Mystical at 8px or 16px. The HUD stays crisp and is never dithered, as today: the post shader only runs on the 3D render.

Colour has a job. Amber/gold marks items and frames. Flame means "look at this". Blood is only for health and danger, and green is only for "in hand / can drop". Everything else is ink at some opacity, so the HUD sinks into the night.

Quiet until needed. Stamina hides at full and status icons sit at 38% opacity until they change state. The target bar only exists in combat. A healthy player walking at night sees one bar, the compass and the hotbar.

Considered and left out: a DayZ-style toolbelt row, because the hotbar already covers quick access per hand. A minimap, because it would undercut the lantern-to-lantern navigation and the dark. Stackable items, because the game deliberately doesn't stack and the concept keeps it that way.
